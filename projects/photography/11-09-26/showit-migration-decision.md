## 1. What the live audit actually found (11 Sept, verified by curl)

### The architecture
Confirmed hybrid. **Money pages are Showit. The blog is WordPress 6.8.8.** Two content systems on one domain, each with its own routing, its own redirect handling and its own idea of what the site contains.

### ✅ The good news, and it corrects a premise
**Showit server-renders its copy.** This matters, because the standard case against Showit is "it is all JavaScript, Google cannot read it." On this site that is **not true**:

| Page | Raw HTML | Visible words with JS disabled | Title | Meta desc | H1 | H2s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `/` | 492 KB | 1,918 | ✅ | ✅ | 1 | 20 |
| `/london-elopement-packages` | 419 KB | 2,971 | ✅ | ✅ | 1 | 17 |
| `/about` | 194 KB | 611 | ✅ | ✅ | 1 | 5 |

Copy, headings and metadata are all in the raw HTML. Google can read these pages. **Showit is not why we rank at position 25.**

### ❌ The real problem, confirmed still live after 5 months
**Not one money page is in any sitemap.**

The sitemap index lists five sitemaps. Between them they advertise 14 blog posts plus this page list:

```
/                        /portfolio/           /gift-card-balance/
/gift-card/              /lightroom-presets/   /free-lightroom-presets/
/checkout/               /cart/                /my-account/
```

Checked against every sitemap on the domain:

| Page | In any sitemap? |
|---|---|
| `/about` | ❌ 0 |
| `/contact` | ❌ 0 |
| `/london-elopement-packages` | ❌ 0 |
| `/london-engagement-photographer` | ❌ 0 |
| `/portrait-photographer-london` | ❌ 0 |

So the sitemap tells Google about the shopping cart, the checkout, the my-account page and some Lightroom presets, and says nothing about a single service page.

**Why it has never been fixed:** the sitemaps are generated by WordPress. WordPress cannot see the Showit pages, because they do not exist inside WordPress. This is not a setting anyone forgot to switch on. It is what the hybrid architecture does. It was flagged in April, flagged again in the 24 August forensics, and it is still exactly the same today.

**Calibration, so this is not overstated:** absent from a sitemap does not mean absent from the index. All five pages return 200 and are linked from the navigation, so Google will have found them by crawling. A sitemap is a discovery and priority hint, not a requirement. This is a real and chronic signal-quality defect. It is not a catastrophe, and fixing it alone would not move rankings.

### Other findings
- **Page weight is heavy.** 492 KB of raw HTML on the homepage. Showit's absolute-positioned layout engine produces very large markup. Page experience is a real but modest Google signal, so treat this as a user-experience and conversion issue first, an SEO issue a distant second.
- **Utility pages are being advertised.** `/cart/`, `/checkout/`, `/my-account/` belong in the sitemap for no one. Minor, but it is noise in the one file we use to tell Google what matters.
- **The live copy is two brand generations out of date.** Homepage meta still reads *"Intentional, artful wedding elopement photography in London, Dubai and beyond, crafted with emotion, elegance, and story."* That is pre-brand-foundation copy, and it carries four banned-crutch words. The Refined Dreamer voice locked on 4 June and the Phase 2 homepage rewrite (Hero locked 5 June, stalled at Block 3) **have never reached the live site.**

## 2. The honest strategic read

### Where Hadi is right
1. **The hybrid is the root cause of a whole class of defects.** Sitemap fragmentation, the June sub-sitemaps-returning-HTML battle that needed Showit Support, the redirect plugin wipe, 3-hop redirect chains, money pages orphaned from the sitemap. These are not separate bugs. They are one architectural decision expressing itself repeatedly.
2. **Contract renewal is a free exit.** Migrating at renewal costs a fraction of migrating mid-term. Passing on this window means committing to another year of the same architecture.
3. **This is the cheapest possible moment in the site's history to migrate.** At ~11 clicks per week there is almost nothing to break. Per the playbook's statistical power rule, a site under ~100 clicks/week has no internal statistical power anyway.
4. **The site does not reflect the brand.** This is the strongest argument and it was not the one made. Months of locked brand foundation work is sitting in the repo, unshipped, because pushing it into Showit was never scheduled. A rebuild is the natural vehicle to finally ship it.

### Where I have to push back
1. **Migration will not fix the rankings.** The playbook's 22 August conclusion stands: 21 months post-rebrand and unrecovered is an **authority problem**, not a technical one. Domain authority and referring domains are why we sit at position 25 for the head terms. A new platform changes none of that. If the expectation going in is "new site, rankings recover," February will be a disappointment and the whole methodology rebuild gets blamed for a mis-set expectation.
2. **The highest-leverage SEO work is not on the website at all.** Money keywords here are map-pack queries. The pack takes 42-44% of local clicks, and GBP is 32% of pack weight with reviews at 20%. **A migration touches none of that.** GBP completeness and review velocity remain the single best-value hours available and they are entirely platform-agnostic.
3. **Opportunity cost is the real risk, not SEO damage.** Click AI Agency is the stated primary priority. Elan is at the partner meeting next week. A site rebuild is a 4 to 8 week attention sink that feels productive while producing no revenue. That is the actual danger here, and it is bigger than any ranking risk.

## 3. The platform question — and the part worth thinking hardest about

Hadi's framing was "move to our own platform." Before choosing one, note where the ranking asset actually lives.

**The blog is already WordPress, and the blog is the content asset.** 14 posts, indexed, ranking, and the long tail they produce is where the majority of query coverage sits.

Which means the lowest-risk move is not "migrate to a new platform." It is:

> **Bring the Showit pages into the WordPress that is already running the blog.**

What that buys:
- **Blog URLs never move.** Zero risk to the only content currently earning anything. This is the single biggest de-risking factor available and no other option offers it.
- **The hybrid dies.** One system, one sitemap, one redirect table, one place to make a change. The sitemap defect fixes itself the moment the pages live in WordPress.
- **Full control**, which is what Hadi asked for. Own hosting, own theme, no annual licence, no support ticket to change a sitemap.
- **A modern build is entirely achievable here.** The "WordPress looks dated" objection is a 2015 objection.

The alternative, a fresh build on Lovable or similar, gives the best visual and performance ceiling but requires migrating the blog too, which puts the one working asset at risk for a benefit that is mostly aesthetic. **Not recommended, but it is Hadi's call and I will build the plan either way.**

## 5. Preserving rankings through the move

Hadi's instinct, keep the copy so we do not lose rank, is right but incomplete. Copy is not what carries rankings through a migration. In order of importance:

1. **URL preservation.** Every URL that currently ranks keeps its exact path. Anything that must change gets a single-hop 301. No chains. We already have documented 3-hop chains and two homepage-dump redirects, so this needs doing properly rather than inherited.
2. **A complete 1:1 redirect map**, built from a full pre-migration URL inventory. Nothing gets left to a catch-all.
3. **Content parity or better.** Same or more content per page. A page that ranks on 2,971 words does not get replaced by 800 words of nicer design.
4. **Metadata carried across**, then improved into Refined Dreamer once stable. Not on day one, so cause and effect stay readable.
5. **Staging, crawl, launch, then verify.** Full crawl before cutover, GSC change monitoring after, daily for two weeks.

Expect a **temporary dip of 2 to 6 weeks** even on a clean migration. At 11 clicks a week that is a rounding error, which is precisely why now is the right time.

1. **Yes, leave Showit.** The hybrid architecture is a chronic defect generator and renewal is a free exit.
2. **Consolidate into the existing WordPress.** Blog URLs never move, the hybrid dies, control is total.
3. **Do not expect a ranking lift.** Migration removes a defect class and unblocks the brand work. Authority and GBP move the rankings, and those are separate jobs already scoped.

5. **Start the prep now, because prep is not build.** The URL inventory and redirect map have to exist regardless, and that work is the same work that fixes today's sitemap defect.
6. **Run the platform-agnostic SEO work in parallel, starting immediately.** GBP, reviews, links, venue brain-dump. None of it waits for a website.

## Resolving the WordPress objection

The objection is fair, and it is really an objection to the WordPress **front end**. But the deeper point is this:

> **The sitemap defect is not a Showit bug. It is what having two systems does.** Any single-system architecture makes it structurally impossible, because the sitemap gets generated from the actual route list at build time rather than by a plugin guessing.

That reframes the choice. The question is not "which platform has the best SEO." It is "which single system do we want."

### The three real options

| | **Astro / Next on VPS** | **Ghost on VPS** | **Headless WordPress** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design ceiling | Unlimited | Good, theme-bound | Unlimited |
| Sitemap defect | Impossible by construction | Impossible | Impossible |
| Page weight vs today's 492 KB | ~50 KB | Light | Light |
| Blog URLs preserved | Yes, by routing | Yes | Yes, trivially |
| Store | Needs rehoming | Not suited | WooCommerce survives |
| Editing | Markdown in this repo | Proper admin UI | WP admin |
| Hosting | His VPS, £0 extra | His VPS, £0 extra | VPS + build host |
| Needs a developer | Him + Jodie | Low | **Yes, and none identified** |
| Risk of recreating a hybrid | None | **High** — marketing pages would need a second system | None |

**Ghost is the trap.** It is lovely for a blog, but bespoke service and portfolio pages would need something alongside it, which rebuilds the exact two-system architecture we are escaping.

**Headless WordPress** solves the design objection cleanly and keeps WooCommerce, but it is the most complex option and there is still no confirmed dev resource (logged 5 June — "Arman" was the voice agent, not a developer).

## Recommendation: Astro, built in this repo, deployed to the VPS

Reasons specific to this situation rather than generic:

1. **Single system, so the sitemap defect cannot recur.** `@astrojs/sitemap` builds the sitemap from the route manifest. If a page exists, it is in the sitemap. That is Hadi's stated must-have, solved architecturally rather than by discipline.
2. **Highest design ceiling of the three.** Nothing is theme-bound. This directly answers the WordPress objection.
3. **492 KB to roughly 50 KB.** Fixes page weight as a side effect.
4. **It matches how he already works.** He runs his business out of this git repo with an AI assistant. Publishing becomes "write the post, commit, auto-deploy" — plausibly a better workflow for him than a CMS admin panel, not a worse one.
5. **£0 hosting** on the VPS he already pays for.
6. **13 posts is a day of migration, not a project.**

### Honest blockers
- **The store needs a decision.** Cleanest answer is to retire WooCommerce entirely and sell the four preset packs through Stripe Payment Links or a Gumroad/Lemon Squeezy page. That removes cart, checkout, my-account and the gift-card plugin from the site completely. Gift cards need their own call.
- **No dev resource.** This is Hadi plus Jodie. Realistic for a 13-post content site with a custom front end, but it should be said out loud rather than assumed.
